Selling Your First Course Without Paying for Three Different Tools

October 29, 2026 · Tool reviewed: Systeme.io

You have a course to sell — maybe it's still a folder of videos and a Google Doc, maybe it's already recorded. Then you start listing what you supposedly need to sell it: a landing page tool, an email service to collect buyers and send lessons, and a course platform to host the videos. Each one has a monthly fee. Each one needs its own login, its own setup weekend, and its own integration with the next one, usually through a third connector tool that also costs money.

For a first product with zero sales, that stack is hard to justify. The fees start immediately; the revenue doesn't. This is the exact situation Systeme.io is built for. It's an all-in-one platform that combines sales funnels and pages, email marketing, and course hosting in a single dashboard — and it has a permanent free plan that is genuinely usable, not a 14-day trial. This article looks at what that means in practice for a solo creator selling a first course, what the free plan actually covers, and — just as important — what changes when you move to a paid plan, because that is where most reviews stay vague.

One dashboard for the whole selling flow

The basic idea is simple: instead of separate subscriptions, you build everything in one place. A typical first-course setup inside Systeme.io looks like this: an opt-in page offering a free sample lesson, an email sequence that follows up with people who didn't buy, a sales page with checkout, and the course itself, delivered lesson by lesson. When someone buys, they're automatically enrolled in the course and tagged in your contact list, because the page, the checkout, the email list, and the course all share the same database. There is nothing to connect.

That matters more than it sounds. With a stitched-together stack, the most common beginner failure isn't design — it's plumbing. The payment goes through but the welcome email doesn't fire, or buyers end up in the wrong list. Having one system removes that whole category of problems, at the cost of using tools that are solid rather than best-in-class in each individual category. For a first product, that's usually the right trade.

1. Pages and checkout without a separate funnel tool

Systeme.io's page builder is drag-and-drop with templates for opt-in pages, sales pages, and full funnels. You can connect your own custom domain even on the free plan, and checkouts run through Stripe or PayPal. Two details are worth knowing. First, Systeme.io charges 0% transaction fees on every plan, including free — you only pay the standard Stripe or PayPal processing fees. Some course platforms quietly take a percentage of each sale on their cheaper plans, which is exactly when you can least afford it. Second, the editor is functional rather than fancy. Reviewers consistently describe it as easy to learn but basic: limited design customization, no page animations. If pixel-perfect branding is your priority, you'll feel the ceiling. If getting a clean, working sales page live this week is the priority, it does the job.

2. Email built in, with unlimited sends

Email is where separate-tool costs usually bite first, because most email services charge by list size and send volume. Systeme.io includes email campaigns and automation in every plan, and sends are unlimited — even on the free plan, which allows up to 2,000 contacts. For a first launch, that combination is generous: you can run a proper launch sequence (announcement, reminders, last-call) without watching a send counter.

The limitation is depth, not volume. The free plan includes only a small number of automation rules and workflows, so your sequences stay simple: tag people when they opt in or buy, send a fixed series of emails. That's enough for a first course. It is not enough for elaborate branching funnels — and by the time you need those, you'll know whether the business justifies paying.

3. Course hosting with drip content and progress tracking

Your course lives in the same system. You upload your videos and files (file storage is unlimited), organize them into modules and lessons, and can release lessons on a drip schedule instead of all at once. Students get progress tracking, so they can see where they left off — a small feature that noticeably affects whether people finish. The free plan includes one course, which sounds restrictive until you remember the target reader of this article: you are selling your first course. One is the correct number to start with.

There's one more built-in feature worth flagging because it's usually an expensive add-on elsewhere: affiliate program management. If you later want other people to promote your course for a commission, you can run that program inside the same platform instead of bolting on another service.

What you actually get for free

The free plan is permanent and requires no credit card. In practical terms, a complete first-course business fits inside it: up to 2,000 contacts, 3 sales funnels (enough for a lead magnet funnel, a sales funnel, and one experiment), 1 course, 1 blog, 1 custom domain, unlimited email sends, and 0% transaction fees. You can realistically make your first sales while paying nothing but payment-processing fees.

The ceilings are real, though. Three funnels won't cover client work or a big product catalog. One course is fine until you want a second. Automation stays basic. And Systeme.io branding appears in places on the free tier. These are "good problems" — you hit them by succeeding — but you should know they're there.

What changes on the paid plans

This is the section that decides whether the platform grows with you, so here are the concrete differences rather than a vague "more features."

Startup ($17/month) is the natural first upgrade. Contacts rise to 5,000, funnels to 10, and courses to 5, and Systeme.io branding is removed. For most solo creators, this is the tier where the free plan's ceilings stop mattering, and it's still cheaper than a single mid-range email subscription elsewhere.

Webinar ($47/month) raises contacts to 10,000, funnels to 50, and courses to 20, and adds automated ("evergreen") webinars — recorded presentations that run like live events and sell your course on a schedule. This is the tier aimed at creators whose main sales engine is webinar funnels rather than email launches.

Unlimited ($97/month) removes nearly all caps — unlimited contacts, funnels, and courses — and adds extras such as sub-accounts and a free migration service if you're moving an existing business over.

All paid tiers are cheaper on annual billing (roughly two months free). One caution: Systeme.io's terms state that subscriptions aren't refundable, so start monthly if you're unsure. You can check the current limits and prices here: Systeme.io pricing

Who this fits — and who it doesn't

Systeme.io fits the creator this article started with: one person, a first course or digital product, a small or nonexistent list, and a strong preference for spending time on the product instead of on tool plumbing and monthly fees. It also fits anyone currently paying for a funnel tool plus an email service plus a course platform and suspecting — correctly — that one system would cover 90% of what they use.

It fits less well if you need advanced design control, complex automation branching from day one, or a large app ecosystem of third-party integrations; the platform is deliberately self-contained, and integrations focus mainly on payment providers. Agencies running many client businesses will also outgrow the structure quickly.

If you're in the first group, the practical next step costs nothing: create the free account, build the sales page for the course you already have, and connect Stripe or PayPal.
